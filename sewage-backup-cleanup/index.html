When a floor drain gurgles, a toilet overflows with no clog in sight, or dark water rises in a basement shower, you are dealing with a sewage backup. Emergency sewage backup cleanup in Commerce City, CO removes the contaminated water, strips out what it soaked, and sanitizes the space. The 24/7 line is open every day, and a crew can be dispatched the same day when available.

Sewage backups most often show up in the lowest part of a house, since that is where the pipes drain out. Basements with a floor drain, a laundry sink or a bathroom below grade are the usual first places the water appears.

Why It Happens

Where A Backup Starts

Water has to go somewhere, and a blocked line sends it back up the lowest opening. The blockage may sit in your own building drain, which runs from the house to the property line, or farther along in the public main that serves the whole street.

Roots, grease, wipes and collapsed older pipe are common blockages in general. Heavy rain can also overload a system. Which one you have decides who fixes the pipe, and it decides what the restoration crew can start on right away.

First Hour

Safety Steps Before The Crew Arrives

1

Stop adding water

Do not flush, run the dishwasher or start a load of laundry. Every gallon you send down the line has nowhere to go.

2

Keep people and pets out

Treat the water as contaminated. Children and pets should stay out of the affected rooms, and nobody should wade through it barefoot.

3

Mind the electricity

If water reaches outlets, a panel or appliances, stay out and call 911 or the electric utility. Cut power only from a dry spot you can safely reach.

4

Call the right line first

If more than one drain is backing up, the public main may be involved, so call the wastewater district before anyone digs or rents a machine.

5

Photograph and wait

Take photos from a dry doorway. Do not carry soaked items through clean rooms or run a household vacuum on the water.

Our Process

What Removal And Sanitizing Involve

The crew sets up in protective gear and starts with extraction. Pumps and extraction units pull the contaminated water out, and the area is sealed with plastic sheeting where needed so the rest of the house stays separate from the work zone.

Next comes removal. Porous materials that soaked up sewage, such as carpet, pad, insulation, the lower part of drywall and stored cardboard, are bagged and discarded. They cannot be made trustworthy again, and keeping them leaves odor and contamination in the house.

Hard surfaces such as concrete, tile and framing are cleaned and treated with sanitizing products suited to sewage. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, moisture meters confirm the readings, and air scrubbers run while demolition dust is in the air. Crews follow ANSI/IICRC S500 as an industry reference for water categories.

Insurance

Sewage Backup And Your Policy

Many standard homeowners policies treat a sewer or drain backup differently from a sudden pipe break. Some carry a sewer backup endorsement, sometimes called water backup coverage, and some do not. Limits and deductibles vary a great deal.

Check your policy or ask your insurer before work starts, then keep photos, receipts and the crew's written scope. Questions about the scope can go to the dispatcher at Call (303) 952-8295. Coverage is decided by your policy, and no restoration company can promise it.

Mistakes that make a backup worse

Running water to flush the line, using a household vacuum on sewage, mixing cleaning products, and keeping soaked carpet for a few days to see if it dries are the usual errors. Each one spreads contamination or hides it behind a wall.

Is emergency sewage backup cleanup available in Commerce City?

Who do I call first, the district or a restoration crew?

If several drains back up together or neighbors have the same problem, call the wastewater district first because the public main may be blocked. Call the restoration line at the same time to start containment and extraction.

Can I clean up sewage myself?

A very small spill on a hard floor is one thing. Anything that soaked carpet, drywall or a basement floor should be treated as contaminated and handled by a crew with protective gear and extraction equipment.

What gets thrown away after a sewage backup?

Porous materials that absorbed the water, such as carpet, pad, insulation, lower drywall and cardboard, are usually discarded. Concrete, tile and framing are cleaned and sanitized.

Does my homeowners policy cover a sewer backup?

It depends on the policy. Many treat backups separately and offer an endorsement for them, so check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep photos and receipts.
