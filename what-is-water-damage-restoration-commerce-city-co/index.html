How a Water Damage Job Works: A Commerce City Guide

A plain definition, the five stages of a job from first visit to rebuild, and how restoration differs from plumbing and waterproofing.

  • Five stages explained
  • ANSI/IICRC S500 named
Emergency Dispatch(303) 952-8295Call Now

By the Commerce City Water Damage Pros team. Published October 26, 2026.

Water damage restoration is the full process of returning a building to its pre-loss condition after water gets in where it does not belong. It covers finding where the water went, removing it, drying every wet material, cleaning what can be saved and rebuilding what cannot.

The industry standard behind this work is ANSI/IICRC S500, which describes how professionals assess water losses and dry structures. Most jobs follow five stages, and each one has a clear purpose.

The Process

The Five Stages From Start To Finish

Small jobs may skip a stage, and large ones repeat some. The order stays the same.

1

Assess

A technician finds the source, checks which materials got wet and takes moisture readings. This sets the scope and shows how far the water traveled behind walls and under floors.

2

Extract

Pumps and wet vacuums pull out standing water. Removing liquid first is far faster than letting equipment evaporate it, so this stage shortens everything that follows.

3

Dry

Air movers and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of drywall, framing, subfloor and concrete. Technicians return to re-read the meters until each material reaches a dry reading.

4

Clean

Surfaces that stay in place are cleaned, and contents are sorted into those worth saving and those to discard. Water from a sewer line or outside flood calls for stricter cleaning and removal of porous materials.

5

Rebuild

Drywall, insulation, flooring, trim and paint are replaced where removal was needed. Some companies do this in house and others hand it to a contractor, so ask who is doing it and when.

Why Order Matters

Why You Cannot Skip Straight To Painting

Drywall, wood and insulation hold water long after the surface feels dry. Painting over a wall that still has a high moisture reading traps that water behind the finish. Mold needs moisture to grow, so a damp wall becomes a problem again.

That is why restoration pairs drying with measurement. A technician records readings on day one and again on later visits, then removes equipment only when the numbers match a dry reference material. Guessing by touch is not reliable.

Choosing Help

What To Expect From A Good Crew

You should get a written scope that lists what will be removed, dried and rebuilt. The scope and price are explained before work starts, and changes are discussed before they are made. Ask what equipment will run, how many days it is expected to stay and how drying will be verified.

Photos and moisture logs are part of the record. They help if you file a claim, and they let you compare what you were told against what happened. Check your policy and ask your insurer about coverage, since a crew cannot decide that for you.

By Water Type

Clean, Gray And Black Water

A supply line break puts clean water in the house at first, but it turns worse the longer it sits. Water from a washing machine or dishwasher is gray water, and it carries soap and some contamination. Sewage and outside flood water are black water and call for removal of porous materials such as carpet padding and drywall.

The type of water changes the cleaning stage and how much gets removed. It rarely changes the first step, which is to stop the source and get the water out.

Where Each Service Fits

FAQ

Common Questions

What is the difference between water damage restoration and water mitigation?

Mitigation is the early part of the job, which means stopping the damage from spreading through extraction and drying. Restoration is the whole process, including cleaning and rebuilding.

How long does water damage restoration take?

Drying commonly takes several days, and rebuilding adds more time depending on how much was removed. The crew can give a realistic schedule after taking moisture readings.

Do I need a plumber or a restoration company?

Often both. The plumber repairs the pipe or fixture, and the restoration crew removes water and dries the structure. If the leak is still running, stop it first.

Is restoration the same as waterproofing?

No. Restoration deals with damage that already happened. Waterproofing is work meant to keep water out in the future.

Who decides what my insurance pays?

Your insurer decides, based on your policy. A crew can supply photos, moisture readings and a written scope for your claim.
