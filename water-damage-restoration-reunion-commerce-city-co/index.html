First Response

Safety Steps In The First Hour

1

Stay out of risky rooms

Do not enter a flooded area if water may be reaching outlets, appliances or the panel. Call your electric utility if you are unsure.

2

Stop the source if safe

Close the shutoff valve at the fixture or appliance, or the main valve, only if it is reachable and dry underfoot.

3

Photograph before moving things

Take wide and close photos of wet walls, floors, ceilings and belongings, with the date on your phone.

4

Treat sewage as contaminated

If the water is from a drain or toilet, keep people and pets away and wait for professional help. A fan or household vacuum is not enough.

5

Call and describe the loss

Extraction And Drying

How Water Is Removed And The Structure Is Dried

A crew starts by finding the extent of the wet area with moisture meters and often a thermal camera, which shows temperature differences behind walls. Standing water comes out with pumps or truck-mounted or portable extractors, and wet carpet pad is usually removed because it dries poorly in place.

Air movers push dry air across surfaces while dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room air. Readings are repeated each visit and drying is complete when materials reach their dry targets, not when they feel dry. The industry reference for this process is ANSI/IICRC S500, a standard and not a certification.

Mold can follow materials that stay damp, which is why wet drywall and trim get checked early.

Insurance

Documenting The Loss For A Claim

Coverage depends on the cause of the water and on your policy terms, so ask your insurer before repairs begin. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope of the work can help you describe what happened and what was done.

Keep receipts and a list of damaged belongings. Ask whether your policy treats a sudden pipe break differently from a slow leak, and whether storm water falls under a different section.
