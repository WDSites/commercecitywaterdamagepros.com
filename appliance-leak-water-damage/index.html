Common Sources

Where Appliance Leaks Start

Washing machine supply hoses are a classic source. They sit under constant pressure, and a split hose can run for as long as nobody is home. An upstairs laundry is worse, since the water finds the ceiling below and runs along joists to unexpected rooms.

Dishwashers leak at the door gasket, the supply line, the drain connection or the pump. Refrigerator ice-maker lines are small tubing that can crack behind the unit, where the damage goes unseen. Water softeners add a drain line and a brine tank to the list.

The pattern is the same everywhere. A modest failure leaks quietly behind or under the appliance, and the subfloor, wall base and cabinet backs soak up the difference.

First Hour

Safety Steps Before You Move The Appliance

1

Stop the supply

Close the shut-off valves at the appliance if you can reach them and they turn. If not, close the main water valve for the house.

2

Unplug only if the floor is dry

If you must stand in water to reach the plug, do not. Switch off the breaker from a dry spot or call your electric utility.

3

Do not pull heavy units through water

A full washer or refrigerator is heavy and the floor is slick. Wait for help if the area is soaked.

4

Photograph before cleanup

Capture the appliance, the hose, the valves, the model label and the wet areas.

5

Call for help

Call the 24/7 line, then your plumber or appliance repair company for the failed part.

Drains And Backups

When The Water Came Back Up

Some appliance water comes from the drain side, not the supply side. A washer standpipe that overflows or a dishwasher that backs up into the sink can put dirty water on the floor. That water is not the same as a clean supply leak.

The South Adams County Water and Sanitation District provides wastewater service for most of the city. If a backup comes up through the appliance line and other fixtures slow down too, ask whether the main line is involved and contact the district. A clog inside your own pipe is yours to clear.

What We Do

How Appliance Leak Drying Works

Crews extract the visible water, then use moisture meters and a thermal camera to follow it behind the unit, into the wall base and beneath flooring. Flooring that has trapped water underneath, such as laminate or vinyl over a subfloor, often needs panels lifted to dry properly.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings are back to normal. The crew works to the ANSI/IICRC S500 industry reference standard for water damage restoration. Drain-side water is treated as contaminated, with more removal and cleaning.

Insurance

Sudden And Accidental Versus Gradual

Insurers often separate sudden and accidental water damage from damage that developed slowly. A hose that bursts and floods a laundry room is usually viewed differently from a drip that rotted a cabinet back over months.

Check your policy for the wording, ask your insurer, and keep your records together. Photos, receipts and the crew's written scope all help. Do not discard a failed hose or part until your insurer has said whether they want to see it.

Prevention

Keep The Next Leak Small

Look behind the washer once or twice a year for bulging hoses and rust at the fittings. Replace old rubber hoses before they fail. Turn the washer valves off if the house will be empty for days. Keep a catch pan under appliances above living space, and move boxes away from the wall base behind the refrigerator.

Is emergency appliance leak water damage cleanup available in Commerce City?

Should I unplug the appliance that leaked?

Only if the floor around it is dry and you can reach the plug safely. If water is on the floor, switch off the breaker from a dry spot or call your electric utility.

Can a slow leak behind an appliance cause damage?

Yes. A slow drip behind a washer, dishwasher or refrigerator can soak the subfloor and wall base for a long time. Moisture meters and a thermal camera find the extent.

What if the water came up from a drain?

Drain-side water is treated as contaminated, so more material is removed and cleaned. If other fixtures also back up, ask the wastewater provider about the main line.

Does insurance pay for an appliance leak?

It depends on your policy and on whether the damage was sudden and accidental or gradual. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
