Standards

Standards, Not Slogans

The restoration industry publishes two standards that shape good work. ANSI/IICRC S500 covers water damage restoration, including how water is categorized by contamination and how structures are dried. ANSI/IICRC S520 covers mold remediation, including containment and verification. We name these as industry standards, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to them.

We do not claim a firm certification, a warranty or insurance-company approval, because none of those claims would be fair to make on a dispatch page. We do say that the crews are licensed and insured, and that the scope and price are explained before any work begins.

What We Do

The Six Services

Work falls into six services. Water mitigation and emergency water removal deal with the water itself. Water damage cleanup and flood damage restoration deal with soiled and contaminated material. Water damage repair rebuilds. Mold remediation is a secondary service for moisture that has already fed growth. The services page explains which one fits.

Honest Limits

What We Do Not Do

Restoration is not plumbing repair, and it is not waterproofing. We will not tell you that cleaning up a flooded basement will keep it from flooding again. We do not post exact prices online, because the scope decides the price. And we do not make health claims about mold, since those questions belong with a physician.

Commerce City

A Local Base

Commerce City is in Adams County. The city's own history page says it incorporated in 1952 and describes the John D. Henderson ranch and trading post of 1859 as the first permanent settlement in the South Platte River valley. Rose Hill, Adams City, Derby and Irondale are among the places in its history. Older neighborhoods often have older plumbing and building materials, so a crew checks what is actually behind the walls.

The South Adams County Water and Sanitation District serves most of the city for water and wastewater, and its office is at 6595 E. 70th Avenue. The City of Commerce City's Building Safety Division handles building permits and can be reached at 303-289-3790. If your project needs a permit, ask that office before work starts.

FAQ

Common Questions

Who answers the phone?

A dispatcher answers, collects the details of the loss and explains what to do safely while a crew is planned. Calls to our line may be recorded for quality and training purposes.

Are the crews licensed and insured?

Yes. Crews are licensed and insured restoration professionals. This site does not publish a licence number, because it does not publish credentials it cannot show.

Do you work with my insurance company?

We provide photos, moisture readings and a written scope you can send to your insurer. Coverage decisions belong to your insurer, so check your policy or ask them.

Do you handle emergencies at night and on weekends in Commerce City?

Yes. The dispatch line is open 24 hours a day, every day, and a crew can be sent the same day when available.
