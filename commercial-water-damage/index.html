A leak in a commercial building is a different kind of problem from the same leak in a house. The space may have several occupants, a landlord, a property manager and an insurer, all with their own questions.

Commercial water damage restoration in Commerce City, CO focuses on what the crew actually does, which is water extraction, structural drying, cleanup and documentation for non-residential buildings. The 24/7 line is open every day, and a crew can be dispatched the same day when available.

This page describes the work, not a list of past clients. If your building has water in it, call and describe the space, and the dispatcher explains the scope and price before work starts.

Roles

Property Managers, Owners And Tenants

In a commercial loss, the first call often belongs to whoever notices the water. Tenants should tell the property manager or owner right away, since the lease usually assigns responsibility for the building shell, the suite interior and contents in different ways.

The manager typically controls the main shut-off, building access and the insurer relationship for the structure. A tenant often has its own policy for contents and business interruption. Read the lease and each policy to see who reports what, and keep each party informed in writing.

First Hour

Safety Steps For Staff And Managers

1

Protect people first

Move staff and visitors away from wet floors. Mark slippery areas and block access to affected rooms.

2

Stop the water

Close the fixture, equipment or area shut-off. If the source is unclear, close the building supply and tell the property manager.

3

Handle electricity carefully

If water reaches outlets, panels or equipment, stay out and call 911 or the electric utility. Cut power only from a dry, reachable spot.

4

Protect records and stock

Lift paper files, electronics and inventory off the floor if it is safe, and photograph everything first.

5

Call the line

Give the dispatcher the address, the source, the area affected and how a crew gets inside after hours.

Our Process

Extraction, Drying And Cleanup In Non-Residential Space

The crew begins with extraction. Pumps and extraction units pull standing water from carpet, tile and concrete slab, and a moisture survey with meters and a thermal camera maps what is still wet in walls, ceilings and under flooring.

Drying equipment is sized to the space. Air movers, dehumidifiers and air scrubbers go in where needed, and readings are logged on each visit until materials are back at normal levels. Industry practice for this work is described in ANSI/IICRC S500.

Cleanup follows. Wet carpet pad, ceiling tile, insulation and drywall that cannot dry in place are removed, and hard surfaces are cleaned. A written scope lists what was extracted, dried and removed so the building owner has a record.

Operating While Drying

Phased Work So A Business Can Keep Running

Drying a whole building at once is not always possible when a business needs to stay open. The crew can plan the work in phases, starting with the worst-hit area and sealing it off with plastic barriers so the rest of the floor stays usable.

Equipment placement, cord routing and noise are discussed up front, and drying can be scheduled around closing hours when access permits. Tell the dispatcher about opening times, alarm codes, key holders and any areas that must stay accessible. Phasing depends on the layout and the amount of water, so the scope is written after the crew has seen the space.

Insurance

Insurance And Business Interruption Wording

Commercial policies vary a lot. Some cover the building, some cover contents, and some include business interruption wording that responds when a covered loss halts operations. Whether a given loss qualifies is a policy question, so check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep the manager and tenant policies separate.

The restoration side supports the file with documentation: dated photos, moisture readings, equipment logs and a written scope with the list of materials removed. Send everything together rather than in pieces, since a complete file moves faster. The dispatcher at Call (303) 952-8295 can explain what the crew will record.
