Dense Neighborhoods

What Water Damage Looks Like In Dense Neighborhoods

In an apartment or condo building, water rarely stays where it starts. A failed supply line in a second-floor unit can drip through the ceiling below, run down inside a shared wall and reach a third unit before anyone sees a stain.

That means the first job is finding the source and every space it touched. A moisture meter and a thermal camera show wet drywall inside walls that look normal, and the drying plan covers each affected unit, not only the one that called.

Upstairs leaks and shared walls

If the water is coming from the unit above, ask that neighbor to shut off their supply and let building management know right away. Shared walls and floor assemblies carry moisture sideways, so check the rooms next to the wet one as well.

Condo associations and the HOA contact

Condo owners should call the association or HOA contact early. Many associations decide who handles the shared walls, risers and common pipes, and they may have a preferred reporting process that affects how repairs are approved.

Renters and building management

Renters should call building management as well as us. The landlord or manager controls access to other units and to shutoff valves, and they will usually want a written record of what the crew finds.

Utilities

Water Utility And Who To Call First

Denver Water serves about 1.5 million people in the city of Denver and many surrounding suburbs. Its emergencies line is 303-628-6801 and its customer care line is 303-893-2444. If water is flowing from a main or from a service line outside your building, that call comes before ours.

Not every Denver address is on Denver Water, so check your bill to see who supplies and bills your property. Inside the building, the rule is simple. Close the nearest shutoff valve, call building management if you rent or own a condo, and then call us for extraction and drying.

Winter

Cold Months Along The Front Range

Denver International Airport, the official weather station, has a 1991 to 2020 snowfall normal of 49.0 inches. That much snow means repeated freezing and thawing on roofs, and meltwater that can back up behind ice and slip under shingles into a ceiling.

Frozen pipes are the other winter cause. Pipes along exterior walls, in unheated garages or in vacant units can freeze, split and then release water when they thaw. If you find a frozen line, shut off the water to that run, open nearby faucets and call before the thaw does damage.

Insurance

Insurance And Records For Renters And Owners

Renters and owners deal with water damage claims differently. In general, a renter's policy covers belongings, while the building owner or association insures the structure. Check your policy and ask your insurer or the association which part is yours.

Take photos of the water, the damage and the source before anything is moved. Keep receipts, the written scope from the crew and any messages from building management. Give your insurer the full set at once, since information sent in pieces slows a claim down.

Denver's own permit pages could not be read for this page, so ask Denver's building department whether a repair needs a permit. Ask any contractor you hire for a copy of the permit when one applies.
