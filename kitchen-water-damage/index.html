Why Kitchens Are Different

How Water Moves Through A Kitchen

Kitchens are built in layers. Cabinet boxes sit on the subfloor, flooring runs up to the toe kick, and the sink, dishwasher and sometimes a refrigerator line all enter through the same few feet of wall. Water from any of them runs under the cabinets and sits where the air cannot reach it.

Cabinet bases made of particleboard swell quickly and rarely return to shape. Plywood boxes tolerate more. Solid-surface and stone counters survive, but the cabinets and floor underneath often do not, and that is where the work is.

First Hour

Safety Steps In A Wet Kitchen

1

Stop the water

Close the valve under the sink, or the dishwasher valve, or the main if you cannot isolate it.

2

Watch the electricity

Kitchens have many outlets at counter height and floor level. Leave wet outlets alone and switch off power only from a dry spot.

3

Empty the base cabinets

Remove dishes, cleaners and food so wet items stop absorbing and the crew can see the cabinet floor.

4

Photograph everything

Take photos of cabinets, flooring, the ceiling below and the source before wiping or removing anything.

5

Call for help

Call the 24/7 line, then a plumber for the failed part.

Remove Or Dry

What Comes Out And What Gets Dried

Toe kicks are often pulled first so air can reach the cavity under the cabinets. Wet drywall behind a backsplash or sink is cut out when it is soft or stained. Vinyl and laminate over a wet subfloor usually come up, because water trapped beneath cannot dry through them.

Tile and stone floors often stay, and the crew dries under them through drilled access points or the cabinet base. Cabinet boxes are kept when they are firm after drying, and removed when they have swollen, delaminated or are held in place by wet material that cannot be reached.

The ceiling below an upstairs kitchen is checked by touch and by moisture meter. A stain alone does not mean the drywall must go, but a sag or soft spot does.

Equipment

How The Dry-Out Works

The crew extracts surface water, then uses moisture meters and a thermal camera to follow it under cabinets and into walls. Air movers push air into the cavities, and dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room. Injection drying is used when a floor system cannot be opened up easily.

Drying is tracked with daily readings and follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 industry reference standard for water damage restoration. When readings are normal, the equipment leaves.

Order Of Repair

Who Works On The Kitchen And In What Order

The sequence matters. The plumber fixes the source first, so the kitchen does not get wet again. The restoration crew then removes damaged material and dries the structure. Only after that do repairs start.

Rebuild follows the opening up: drywall and backsplash first, then subfloor repair if needed, then cabinets and counters, then flooring, then paint and trim. Ordering new cabinets can take time, so decide early whether the old ones are salvageable. Ask the city's building department whether cabinet or plumbing work needs a permit before it starts.

Insurance

Kitchen Claims, Sudden Or Gradual

Insurers usually look at whether the loss was sudden or built up over time. A supply line that failed overnight is treated differently from a slow drip under a sink that rotted a cabinet base.

Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies. Photograph before any demolition, and keep the plumber's invoice together with the crew's written scope.

Avoid These

Kitchen Mistakes That Cost More

Do not caulk the base of the cabinets to seal the floor while it is still wet. Do not lay new flooring over a floor that has not been checked. And do not wait on a soft spot, since a wet subfloor can keep the room damp long after the surface looks fine.

FAQ

Common Questions

Is emergency kitchen water damage restoration available in Commerce City?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open every day, and a crew can be dispatched the same day when available. The dispatcher explains the scope and price before work starts.

Do cabinets have to be replaced after water damage?

Not always. Firm plywood cabinets can often be dried and kept. Swollen particleboard bases usually need replacing, and a moisture meter helps decide.

Can the kitchen floor be saved?

Tile often can, with drying underneath. Vinyl and laminate over a wet subfloor usually come up so the subfloor can dry.

Who fixes the leak, the crew or a plumber?

A plumber fixes the failed line or fixture. The restoration crew handles extraction, tear-out and drying.

Do I need a permit for kitchen repairs after water damage?

The city pages state no specific rule for water repair. Call the Building Safety Division at 303-289-3790 to ask whether cabinet or plumbing work needs a permit.

What if the kitchen is on the second floor?

The ceiling below is checked for moisture and sagging. A stain alone does not always mean replacement, but a soft or sagging ceiling does.
