A basement is the lowest point of the house, so water from a plumbing leak, a failed sump pump, a window well or the ground itself ends up there. It also holds the furnace, water heater, laundry and stored belongings, which turns a small leak into a larger loss.

This page covers the cleanup of finished and unfinished basements. For surface flooding from a storm or a street, see flood damage restoration.

Where It Comes From

Five Ways Water Gets Into A Basement

Seepage is groundwater pushing through the floor slab, the cove joint where floor meets wall, or cracks in a foundation wall. It tends to show up after long wet spells and is rarely sudden. A window well fills with rain or snowmelt, and water finds its way through the window frame or over the sill.

Pipes cause a different pattern. A supply line, a water heater or a washer hose releases water from one spot and the damage radiates from there. Sump pump failure can look like seepage, but the pit is full or the pump is silent. Sewage backs up from a floor drain and is a separate and more serious situation.

Matching the symptom to the source matters, because the fix for a leaking pipe is a plumber and the fix for a drain backup may be the wastewater district.

Safety first in a wet basement

Do not step into standing water if outlets, the panel, a freezer or any plugged-in device is in it. Water and electricity can be deadly together. If you cannot reach the panel while standing on a dry surface, call 911 or the electric utility and wait. If you smell gas, leave the house.

How Water Travels

Concrete, Framing And Where It Hides

Concrete looks solid and still takes in water. A wet slab can hold moisture for a long time, and it releases it back into the room as humidity. Water also runs along the bottom plate of framed walls, wicks up drywall by an inch or two, and settles into insulation.

In a finished basement, the damage hides behind paneling, wallpaper, baseboards and built-ins. A thermal camera and a moisture meter trace the wetness behind surfaces before anything is cut.

Egress window wells deserve a look because they are an opening in a foundation wall. Debris in the well, a cracked liner or a missing cover lets water collect and leak in around the window frame.

Our Process

Extraction, Demolition And Drying

Standing water comes out first with submersible pumps and extraction units. Then the crew removes materials that cannot be dried: soaked carpet pad, wet insulation and drywall cut a few inches above the visible water line. Stripping wet drywall is not destruction for its own sake. It opens the wall cavity so air can reach the studs and the bottom plate.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and readings are taken at each visit. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the industry reference standard that drying work is measured against. Basement air tends to be damp even without a loss, so dehumidification is a larger part of the job than in an upstairs room.

Unfinished basements are simpler. Concrete walls and floors are cleaned and dried, and stored items are sorted. Finished basements add drywall, flooring and trim to the rebuild, which water damage repair can handle after drying.

Insurance

Where Basement Water Fits On A Policy

Insurers draw lines between water that comes from a plumbing failure, water that seeps up through the ground, and water from a sewer or drain backup. Each can be treated differently and some need an added endorsement. Check your policy or ask your insurer before assuming a basement loss is covered or not.

Sudden events are generally handled differently from long-term seepage. Keep photos, receipts and the crew's written scope.

Mistakes and prevention

Do not run a household vacuum on wet floors, and do not leave wet cardboard and drywall in place hoping it dries. Keep window wells clear, test a sump pump before storm season, and store belongings on shelves instead of the floor. If the water is a one-time event, fix the cause. If it keeps coming back, ask a plumber or foundation contractor about the path.

Common Questions

Is emergency basement water damage cleanup available in Commerce City?

Yes. The 24/7 line is open every day, and a crew can be dispatched the same day when available. The dispatcher explains the scope and price before any work starts.

How do I tell seepage from a pipe leak?

Seepage tends to appear along the base of walls and the floor edge after wet weather. A pipe leak starts at one spot and spreads outward. A moisture meter and the crew's inspection confirm which one it is.

What should I do if my sump pump fails?

Do not touch the pump or wiring if there is water around outlets. Call for extraction, and have a plumber look at the pump and the discharge line once the area is safe.

Do wet drywall and insulation have to come out?

Usually, if they stayed wet. Drywall is cut above the water line and insulation is removed so the wall cavity can dry. Firm drywall that was briefly wet may be dried in place.

Can a basement be dried without removing the floor?

Concrete is cleaned and dried in place. Carpet pad and some flooring over it do come out, because they hold water against the slab.

Who handles a sewer backup in a floor drain?

If the problem is in the public main, the wastewater district handles that part. If it is in your own line, a plumber does. The restoration crew handles the cleanup in the basement.
