Where It Sits

A Mix Of Single-Family Homes And Shared Buildings

One neighborhood directory describes Belle Creek as single family homes with attached garages, plus townhouses and apartments along Belle Creek Boulevard, in Colonial inspired architecture. The same directory says it connects south toward 104th Avenue and east toward Reunion, in northern Commerce City.

Real-estate directories are a general guide only. Your own building type is what matters when water appears, so describe it plainly on the call.

Practical tie: with housing from roughly that period, a crew traces a leak to its actual source, whether a water heater, an appliance connection or a supply line, instead of assuming.

Shared Walls

Townhouses, Apartments And Who To Notify

In a townhouse or apartment building, water often moves through a shared wall or ceiling and reaches a neighbor's unit. If you own a townhouse, tell your HOA or property manager early, since some buildings have rules about who handles common walls, roofs and shutoffs.

Renters should notify the landlord or property manager in writing and keep a copy. Ask who controls the building shutoff and whether neighbors are affected, then photograph what you can see in your own unit.

A single-family house is simpler in one way and harder in another. Everything is yours, but a burst pipe or an appliance leak in a garage or laundry closet can run unseen for a while.

Safety

First Response And Safety

These steps apply to any building type.

  1. Stay out of standing water if it may be touching outlets, appliances or the panel. Call your electric utility if you are unsure.
  2. Shut off the water at the fixture or the main valve only if it is reachable and the floor around it is dry.
  3. Do not flip breakers while standing in water. Wait for a qualified electrician.
  4. Photograph the wet areas, then move small valuables to a dry spot.
  5. Treat water from a drain or toilet as contaminated and keep people away. A household vacuum or fan does not make it safe.
  6. Call (303) 952-8295 and describe the address, the source and how far the water has spread.
The Drying Process

Extraction, Air Movers And Moisture Readings

Crews map wet areas with moisture meters and a thermal camera, then pull water out with extractors or pumps. Wet carpet and pad are often lifted and drying equipment is placed where readings stay high.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until materials meet their dry targets, with readings checked on each visit. The industry reference is ANSI/IICRC S500, which describes how water damage drying is planned and documented.

In shared buildings, the drying plan may need access to a neighbor's unit or a wall cavity on the other side. Notifying the property manager early helps.

Insurance

Records That Help Describe The Work

Coverage depends on the cause and your policy terms, so ask your insurer before repairs begin. The Colorado Division of Insurance says burst pipes are generally covered under homeowners and renters policies, and lists 303-894-7490 for questions.

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope can help. An HOA master policy and your own policy may split responsibility for walls and contents, so ask both.
