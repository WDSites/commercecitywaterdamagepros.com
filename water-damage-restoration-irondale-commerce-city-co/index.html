This page covers water damage restoration in Irondale, a small part of Commerce City, Colorado. It explains what happens after a pipe failure, leak, storm or backup in a home or a business space. call (303) 952-8295 and the dispatcher will confirm availability for your address.

Where It Sits

A Mixed Residential And Industrial Area

One neighborhood directory describes Irondale as mostly ranch style homes, some of them among industrial sites. Lots run from about half an acre to small fenced yards, and many original homes have stood since the 1950s. The same source places it across the highway from the Rocky Mountain Arsenal refuge.

Directories are written for home shoppers, so read this as a loose description. The useful takeaway is that a house and a shop can sit close together, and each is handled differently.

Irondale Local Notes

The Commerce City historical society says Irondale was first settled in 1889 and named for a foundry opened that year. It was incorporated as a town in 1924 and unincorporated in the 1930s. Its early main road, Irondale Road, is now 88th Avenue. If you give a street address, the dispatcher can work from it, so use the current street name.

The official city history says the 1952 Commerce Town incorporation included Irondale among its communities. That makes Irondale part of Commerce City, so city permit rules apply to projects there.

The 2013 flood destroyed the former state fish hatchery's main building near Dupont at 88th Avenue and I-76. That is a single documented event near a nearby highway junction, and it is not evidence that Irondale homes flooded.

National Weather Service Denver normals put the first freeze around October 19 and the last snowfall around April 28. Pipes in unheated spaces such as sheds and detached garages deserve attention during that stretch, and snow melt can find a roof leak. The Colorado Division of Insurance says burst pipes are generally covered under homeowners and renters policies, so contact your insurer.

Possible Situations

Water Damage That Fits Homes And Shops

In a house, the usual possibilities are a burst pipe, a leaking appliance, a failed water heater or a roof leak after a storm. A frozen pipe can split during a cold snap and release water once it thaws.

A shop or small commercial space adds floor drains, restrooms and equipment lines. The commercial water damage page explains how business losses are handled.

First Response

Safety Steps For Irondale Properties

  1. Stay out of flooded areas where outlets, cords or equipment may be energized.
  2. Shut off the water only if it is safe and reachable.
  3. Never flip breakers while standing in water.
  4. Treat sewage as a professional job. Fans and a household vacuum are not enough.
  5. Photograph the damage and any affected stock or equipment before moving it.
  6. Call the line and describe the building.
The Process

Extraction And Drying Equipment

Extractors remove standing water from carpet, slab and floor coverings. Moisture meters and a thermal camera locate wet wall bases and hidden pockets.

Air movers and dehumidifiers then dry the structure and the readings are repeated. The ANSI/IICRC S500 industry reference standard guides this work, and it is a reference and not a certification.

The EPA mold guide advises drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours, so prompt action matters.
